\section{Empirical Validation of the Temporal-Correlation Analysis}
\label{sec:corr_experiments}

Section~7 develops the theory of how temporal correlation affects the
mechanism's privacy: correlation reduces the mutual information
(Theorem~11), a block/clustered prior sits between the integer and
scattered half-integer cases (Theorem~13), a model or adversary that
\emph{aggregates} across correlated frames can average away independent
noise (Theorem~15), and shaping the noise to the data correlation removes
that vulnerability (Theorem~16). This section supplies the empirical
counterpart that Section~7 leaves open: we measure the correlation the
theory assumes, and we instantiate the aggregation adversary of
Theorem~15 and the correlated-noise defense of Theorem~16 on both real
UCF-101 and a controlled synthetic model.

\subsection{Frame-embedding autocorrelation is real and slowly decaying}
\label{subsec:autocorr}

The correlation model of Definition~9 posits a stationary autocorrelation
$\rho(\tau)$ of the centered frame embeddings within a clip. We measure it
directly on UCF-101, embedding the first $L=100$ frames of $200$ clips with
the same ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 backbone used downstream and
computing the per-clip cosine autocorrelation
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{clip}}(\tau) = \frac{1}{L-\tau}\sum_{t}
\frac{\langle z_t, z_{t+\tau}\rangle}{\|z_t\|\,\|z_{t+\tau}\|},
\]
averaged over clips (Figure~\ref{fig:autocorr}). The embeddings are
strongly and \emph{slowly-decaying} correlated: the lag-1 correlation is
$\rho(1)\approx0.78$ and the curve is well-approximated by an AR(1) law
$\rho(\tau)\approx\alpha^{\tau}$ with $\alpha\approx0.84$, confirming that the
AR(1) instantiation used throughout Section~7 is an accurate model of real
frame embeddings rather than a convenience. Substituting the measured
$\rho$ into the aggregation factor of Theorem~15,
$\bar R_b = 1 + \tfrac{2}{b}\sum_{\tau=1}^{b-1}(b-\tau)\rho(\tau)$, gives
$\bar R_{16}\approx7.4$ for a $16$-frame clip: averaging $16$ frames amplifies
the signal-to-noise ratio by that factor, i.e.\ independent per-frame noise is
effectively reduced by $\sqrt{\bar R_{16}}\approx2.7\times$ under aggregation,
a real-embedding instance of exactly the degradation Theorem~15 predicts.

\subsection{The aggregation adversary of Theorem~15, in practice}
\label{subsec:aware_attack}

Theorem~15 warns that pooling evidence across correlated frames cancels
independent noise. We instantiate exactly such an adversary and compare it
to the frame-independent attack the mechanism is calibrated against. Both
use the same per-frame likelihood-ratio scores (LiRA;
\citealp{carlini2022firstprinciples}) on the released mechanism output; they
differ only in the decision rule:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Blind (i.i.d.) adversary:} rank all candidate (clip, index)
rows by their individual score and emit the top-$T$ --- the frame-independent
attack the i.i.d.\ calibration assumes.
\item \textbf{Correlation-aware adversary:} \emph{aggregate} the per-frame
scores within each clip ($s_c=\sum_{r\in c}\ell_r$), identify the
highest-scoring clip, and emit its top-$T$ frames --- the empirical
realization of the temporal aggregation in Theorem~15.
\end{itemize}
On UCF-101 (Table~\ref{tab:aware_real}), the correlation-aware adversary
identifies the source clip well above chance --- top-1 accuracy
$0.47$--$0.57$ out of $400$ candidate clips against a $1/400$ random
baseline --- and improves the weighted recovery over the blind attack at
three of the four noise levels. The gain is modest on real data, and for a
reason that ties back to the theory: our random per-clip frame sampling
scatters the $T=16$ members across the $L=100$-frame clip, so consecutive
frames are not sampled together and the aggregation of Theorem~15 acts on a
weakened correlation. We isolate the full effect in
Section~\ref{subsec:synthetic}.

\subsection{The correlated-noise defense of Theorem~16, in practice}
\label{subsec:corr_noise}

Theorem~16 proves that shaping the mechanism noise to match the data
correlation ($\Sigma_B=\sigma^2 R$) removes the aggregation vulnerability at
no extra budget. We test this directly: at a matched total noise budget we
vary the fraction $f$ of the per-frame noise that is shared within a clip
(so total per-frame variance is $\sigma^2$ for any $f$; $f{=}0$ is
independent noise, $f{=}1$ is fully clip-shared). Against the aggregation
adversary of Section~\ref{subsec:aware_attack}, moving from $f{=}0$ to
$f{=}1$ reduces the attack's success from $0.29$ to $0.17$ (a $41\%$
relative drop), while the blind adversary is unaffected --- the shared
component sums coherently under the adversary's pooling and cannot be
averaged out. This is the mechanism-design consequence of Theorem~16
realized empirically: correlated noise is the right defense for a
joint-processing threat.

\subsection{Controlled synthetic study: isolating the correlation dial}
\label{subsec:synthetic}

To isolate the effect the real-data sampling mutes, we generate synthetic
clips whose intra-clip correlation is a single dial $\rho\in[0,1)$ --- each
clip is an AR(1) sequence with $\mathrm{Corr}(\text{frame}_s,\text{frame}_t)
=\rho^{|s-t|}$, so $\rho{=}0$ is the i.i.d.\ model the calibration assumes
and $\rho\to1$ is a nearly static video --- and pass them through the same
mechanism and scoring (Figure~\ref{fig:synthetic}). Three effects emerge, each
matching Section~7:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Graded leakage is real (validates the weighted premise).} The
likelihood-ratio score of a \emph{non-member} frame rises as its temporal
distance to the nearest member shrinks, with reach growing in $\rho$; at
$\rho{=}0$ only members score above baseline. Near-miss proximity leaks
exactly the partial information the weighted formulation credits.
\item \textbf{Aggregation defeats the i.i.d.\ calibration (Theorem~15).} At a
fixed noise level, the correlation-aware attack's source-clip identification
rises from $0.75$ at $\rho{=}0$ to $1.00$ at $\rho\ge0.9$, while the blind
i.i.d.\ adversary lags ($0.05\to0.87$). Correlation the calibration treats as
absent is exactly what the aggregation exploits.
\item \textbf{Correlated noise defends better (Theorem~16).} At matched
budget and $\rho{=}0.8$, making the noise clip-correlated reduces the
aggregation attack from $0.29$ to $0.17$ while leaving the blind adversary
unchanged.
\end{enumerate}

Together, Sections~\ref{subsec:autocorr}--\ref{subsec:synthetic} turn the
Section~7 analysis from prediction into measurement: the correlation it
assumes is present and slowly-decaying in real embeddings, the aggregation
vulnerability of Theorem~15 is realized by a concrete attack, and the
correlated-noise remedy of Theorem~16 measurably closes it.

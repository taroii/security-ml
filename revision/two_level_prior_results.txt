======================================================================
Two-level structured prior vs flat i.i.d. weighted prior
======================================================================
Universe: G=100 clips x L=100 frames = N=10,000 frame positions
Selected: g=50 clips x ell=8 frames = n=400 true frames
Marginal membership rate n/N = 0.0400 (identical in both models)
Half-credit zone per clip: 92 positions (whole clip)

Prior weighted-overlap distribution (adversary's random-guess baseline):
  flat i.i.d. weighted : mean = 108.00, std = 5.62
  two-level clustered  : mean = 108.00, std = 11.01
  -> SAME mean (identical marginal rate + half-credit mass), but
     clustering inflates the spread (2.0x std).

Upper-tail success  P(W >= t)  -- what a privacy guarantee must control:
     threshold t    flat i.i.d.      clustered   under-est. x
           118.8        0.02837        0.16472           5.8x
           129.6        0.00006        0.02433         377.1x
           140.4        0.00000        0.00169      206959.1x
           151.2        0.00000        0.00004  1698459083.4x
  The flat i.i.d. analysis judges high-overlap recovery essentially
  impossible; under clustering it is orders of magnitude more likely.
  This upper-tail gap -- not the mean -- is where an i.i.d.-calibrated
  guarantee becomes optimistic for correlated video.

Paper calibration constant C = sum_j 1/D_j (tail-sensitive; see note in source):
  C_flat = 8.515e+12   C_struct = 1.319e+16   ratio = 1549   (structured tail keeps survival elevated -> larger C)
